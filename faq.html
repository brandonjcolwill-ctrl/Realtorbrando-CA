FAQ

Questions, answered straight.

The things buyers and sellers ask me most — in plain English, no jargon.

How much down payment do I need as a first-time buyer?

In Canada the minimums are set by law: 5% on the first $500,000 of the price, 10% on the portion between $500,000 and $999,999, and 20% on homes priced at $1 million or more. Anything under 20% down requires mortgage default insurance, which gets added to your mortgage.

What is the FHSA and how does it work?

The First Home Savings Account lets you contribute up to $8,000 per year (lifetime max $40,000). Contributions are tax-deductible going in and tax-free coming out when used for your first home — and unlike some programs, you never repay it. If you haven’t opened one yet, it’s usually the first account to fund.

What is the Home Buyers’ Plan?

The HBP lets first-time buyers withdraw up to $60,000 from their RRSP tax-free toward a home purchase. The catch: you repay it over 15 years. You can use the HBP and the FHSA on the same purchase.

Why do people say Toronto has a ‘double’ land transfer tax?

Buy inside the City of Toronto and you pay both the provincial land transfer tax and Toronto’s municipal land transfer tax. On a $700,000 home that’s roughly $20,950 combined — due in cash on closing day. The good news: eligible first-time buyers get rebates of up to $4,000 provincially and $4,475 on the Toronto portion.

What will I pay at closing besides the down payment?

Budget for land transfer tax (see above), legal fees (roughly $1,500–$2,500), title insurance, and adjustments for prepaid property taxes or condo fees. A safe rule of thumb is 1.5–4% of the purchase price on top of your down payment.

Should I get pre-approved before looking at homes?

Yes. A pre-approval tells you exactly what you can afford, locks a rate while you shop, and makes your offer far more credible to sellers. It costs nothing and takes a day or two with a mortgage broker or bank.

How do you get paid when I buy a home?

In most GTA transactions, the seller’s brokerage offers compensation to the buyer’s brokerage through the MLS system — so buyers typically don’t pay their agent directly out of pocket. We’ll go over exactly how compensation works in your situation before you sign anything.
