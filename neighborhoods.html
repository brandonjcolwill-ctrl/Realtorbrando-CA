Neighborhood guides

Know the street before you buy it.

The GTA isn’t one market — it’s dozens. Here’s a straight-talking overview of the areas I work most, and what buyers should know about each.

Downtown Toronto

Condos, career energy, everything walkable.

Known for the Harbourfront, King West and the Financial District. Condo living dominates — maintenance fees, building amenities and layouts vary widely, so comparing buildings matters as much as comparing units.

North York

A city within the city.

Known for the Yonge–Sheppard corridor and Mel Lastman Square. Housing runs from high-rise condos to established detached streets, and subway access keeps it popular with commuters heading downtown.

Scarborough

Room to grow, value to find.

Known for the Bluffs, Rouge Park and some of the GTA’s most attainable detached homes on generous lots. A strong pick for buyers who want space without leaving Toronto.

Etobicoke

Suburban calm, minutes from the city.

Known for the Kingsway, the Mimico waterfront and Islington Village. A mix of post-war bungalows, established family streets and newer builds — with GO and subway links into downtown.

Vaughan

New-build territory, built around transit.

Known for the Vaughan Metropolitan Centre and rapid growth along the subway extension. Lots of newer construction — great for buyers who want modern finishes, with builder reputations worth researching.

Markham

Top schools, tech employers, strong community.

Known for Unionville’s historic main street and highly regarded schools. A competitive market of detached homes and townhouses — well-priced listings tend to move quickly here.
